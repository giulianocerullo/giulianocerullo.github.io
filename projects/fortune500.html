Staying on Top - Fortune 500 Data Analysis in Python

Context
EM Normandie Business School • AI-Powered Data Analysis using Python & Agile
Type
Academic Project
Period
Oct 2026 – Nov 2026

Overview

Exploratory data analysis in Python of 51 years of Fortune 500 rankings (1955–2005, 25,500 company-years), asking whether revenue growth and profitability are associated with staying on the list five years later, and whether staying on top has become harder over time.

The project was a team project for AI-Powered Data Analysis using Python & Agile at EM Normandie Business School, delivered in two outputs:

Slide deck .pdf fileFive-slide presentation of the research question, approach and main findingsOpen PDF Notebook GitHub repositoryFull analysis with code, charts and interpretations, plus a summary of the key findingsOpen on GitHub

Key Tasks

  • Data cleaning in pandas: missing profits, duplicate company names, sanity checks on 25,500 rows
  • Feature engineering: profit margin, size groups, loss flag, year-over-year revenue growth and five-year retention built with merges
  • Exploratory analysis with matplotlib and seaborn: distributions, outliers, time trends and heatmaps by decade and size group
  • Detected a structural break in 1995, when Fortune added service companies to the list, and corrected the retention analysis for it
  • Tested whether results hold within each size group to rule out a pure size effect

Core Insights

Staying on top got harder: five-year retention fell from about 83% (1950s–1970s) to 70–74% (1980s–2000), even among the top 100 companies

Losses became far more common: from under 5% of companies before 1980 to peaks of 31.8% in 1993 and 24.2% in 2003, while the median margin stayed at 4–5%

Profitability matters more than growth: growing and profitable companies stayed on the list 83% of the time, against 56% for those that were neither, with the gap holding in every size group

Larger companies grow more slowly: median annual growth of 7.1% for ranks 1–100 against 9.7% for ranks 401–500
